Patient concerns

Common concerns aligners may help with

Clear aligners are transparent, removable trays that gradually move teeth into improved alignment — often chosen as a more discreet alternative to traditional braces.

Not every case is suitable for clear aligners. Some bite problems or complex movements may need alternative orthodontic care. A consultation helps identify the best option.

  • Crowded teeth
  • Gaps between teeth
  • Mild to moderate tooth misalignment
  • Relapse after previous braces or orthodontic treatment
  • Smile appearance concerns that require guided tooth movement

Common questions

Before you begin

Clear aligners are designed to be discreet. Most people may not notice them during normal conversation.

Aligners should usually be removed while eating and drinking anything other than water. This helps protect the trays and maintain hygiene.

Treatment time depends on case complexity, patient compliance, and the number of aligners required. Your clinician will estimate the timeline after assessment.

You may feel pressure or mild discomfort when starting a new tray. This usually means the aligners are guiding tooth movement and often settles after a few days.

Yes. Retainers are usually recommended after aligner treatment to help maintain the final tooth position.
